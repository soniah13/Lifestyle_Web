# Life Web

> **Welcome to your 2.0. Your Day 1 can be any day.**
>
> Take your time. When you're ready, proceed.

Life Web is a visual space for finding your way back to yourself, one small action at a time.

Sometimes life gets overwhelming. You can feel stuck for a while, and when you're ready to move again, everything feels like too much and you don't know where to start. I started building Life Web on a day I felt that way. I wanted something that could help me stop watching life pass by, reconnect with the person I want to become, and begin doing things again.

Instead of putting your whole life into one endless to-do list, Life Web lets you map the different parts of your life as an interconnected web. At the centre is **you**. Around you are the things you care for, maintain, explore, learn and hope to build.

## The idea

Your life has more layers than you may realise. Work, health, home, friendships, hobbies, finances, learning and future plans all connect. When everything lives in your head, it can feel impossible to know where to begin.

Life Web helps you lay those layers out, turn branches into practical tasks, and see how small actions contribute to the bigger picture. It isn't about doing everything every day or becoming productive every minute. It is about understanding where you are, choosing what matters now, and moving at your own pace.

## How to begin

### 1. Start with your real life

List what you are already doing. Include the big things and the small things: preparing for class, applying for a job, cooking, showering, taking a walk, reading a few pages, drawing, meeting a friend, or watching a movie and resting.

You don't need to judge or improve everything you record. Start by noticing your life as it is.

### 2. Build your web

Create branches for the areas that make up your life. Add sub-branches for projects, interests, habits and responsibilities. Keep it personal: your web should reflect your life, not somebody else's idea of what a successful life looks like.

### 3. Find one small next step

Open a branch and choose a task you can realistically begin. Make it specific and manageable. For example, instead of *get better at circuit design*, try *simulate one basic LED circuit*.

### 4. Move at your own pace

Mark tasks as you make progress. Add new nodes, change your plans, pause something, or come back to it later. Unfinished tasks are information, not proof that you have failed.

### 5. Look back and notice your growth

Over time, your web can help you see the effort, knowledge, routines and interests that make up your life. Notice which areas you are nurturing and which ones you want to give attention to next. Progress does not have to look the same in every area.

## What makes Life Web different?

- **A visual life map:** Explore your life through connected branches rather than a long, flat task list.
- **Actionable tasks:** Move from a big goal to a small next step you can actually take.
- **Your space, your way:** Add and organise nodes around the life you are living and the future you want to build.
- **A personal universe:** The planned cosmic dark mode represents your own world of interconnected goals, habits and possibilities.
- **Progress without pressure:** Use the web to notice patterns and growth, not to measure your worth by how much you complete.

## The experience I'm building towards

Life Web is currently a prototype, and it will grow as I learn what makes it genuinely helpful. The next steps include:

- Personal accounts and authentication.
- A welcoming sign-in experience with the message **“Welcome to your 2.0. Your Day 1 can be any day.”**
- A one-time *welcome to your universe* animation when someone creates an account for the first time.
- Private, account-specific storage so each person can return to their own web across sessions and devices.
- A smoother, more accessible interactive canvas and a calm, space-inspired dark mode.

Until account-based storage is implemented and verified, treat the current prototype as a local experiment. Keep backups of information you care about, and avoid entering highly sensitive personal information.

## Core belief

You are in control of your life, even when finding your next step feels difficult. You don't have to become a completely different person overnight, and you don't have to start on a Monday, at the beginning of a month, or in a new year.

**Your Day 1 can be any day.**

Take your time. Start by noticing the life you already have. When you're ready, choose one small step and proceed.
